# Rocket Raja: 1,000 cash unlockables blueprint

Updated: 1 October 2026. Planning only.

Cash-only progression is selected. [Economy Specification v1](Economy%20Specification%20v1.md) and the main catalogue contain the current prices and Fame formula, with no completed-prestige requirements for cash upgrades. The x6 opening bonus is retained. Numerical balance remains subject to prototype tuning; the [decision record](Cash-only%20Economy%20Alternative%20v1.md) preserves the superseded comparison.

The user confirmed designing all 1,000 distinct cash unlockables now, approximately three months of major authored progression, and a typical 1–2 prestiges per day after the opening. First prestige targets are 6–10 elapsed hours with mixed play or 2–3 hours of sustained active play, without IAP or ads. Talent opens at first prestige. Cash purchases remain manual with Buy All throughout; automatic buying is excluded.

Confirmed regular-player baseline: 15–25 total active minutes per day after opening day, including management and rebuilding. The selected cash-only baseline has been simulated under reference and alternative policies; results are conditional, not human-play validation.

Confirmed first-rebuild direction: use x6 opening income, prioritizing roughly 3–4x faster matched check-ins and accepting faster sustained recovery. This is an opening coefficient, not a repeatedly stacking x6 reward.

This document allocates the catalogue and explains each family. The companion [catalogue](1000%20Unlockables%20Catalogue.md) supplies all 1,000 IDs, names, numerical prices/effects, and individual prerequisites for selected cash-only progression. ## Product profit: 480 entries

Keep the eight current products as the working roster: Phuljhari, Anar, Chakri, Rockets, Small crackers, Cracker strings, Aerial shots, and Celebration boxes. Each gets 60 profit entries distributed throughout the earning curve.

Use six thematic series per product, ten later upgrades in each series. Candidate themes are packaging, display, supplier quality, repeat customers, signature ranges, and festival demand. Themes provide naming context; prices and economic roles determine placement. Do not place all ten steps of one theme together by default.

The mathematical role is periodically changing which product investment is attractive. Success requires selected early-product upgrades to become useful again after later products appear. A huge multiplier on negligible output is not automatically an interesting purchase. Check direct contribution and total marginal return at each authored price band.

Confirmed: earlier products should periodically become leading earners again. The product draft allocates 80 signatures and 400 supporting purchases within the 480 PP entries. V1 prices every entry; each family's first signature gives x2 and the later nine give x8, replacing its supporting effect. Contribution changes are measured in the coupled model; this does not prove every signature creates an interesting decision.

Signature upgrades use fixed, previewable multipliers. Never calculate their effect from the current leading product's income or dynamically reduce a card's benefit to preserve an intended rotation. Reaching a signature milestone should create a competitive investment opportunity; it does not guarantee first place under every card history or purchase order.

Do not make all 480 entries ×2 automatically. The combined multiplier budget must be designed with level growth, milestones, global upgrades, and permanent bonuses. Even modest repeated multipliers accumulate substantially across 60 entries.

## Product level cost: 128 entries

Each product receives 16 discounts on its level purchases during the current run. These are separate from its permanent Talent discount and from discounts on buying named cash unlockables.

Prototype a cumulative discount curve with explicit incremental gains per purchase rather than 16 identical large percentage cuts. Show the next purchase's resulting price reduction. Earlier discounts remain valuable after later ones are bought. Derive the final rule together with permanent discounts so a combined cap does not erase the benefit of future card duplicates.

## Tapping: 160 entries

There are 120 tap-profit entries and 40 tap-level-cost entries. Interleave them with product upgrades across the complete progression horizon.

The user confirmed worthwhile ordinary tapping throughout the run, with stronger skill bursts. The [Active Play and Skills Draft](Active%20Play%20and%20Skills%20Draft.md) defines a candidate shop-income link that grows with product income and tap investment. The catalogue has 60 direct-profit TD steps and 60 linked-income TL steps. TD multipliers affect the direct component while their owned count modestly improves bounded tap mastery; they do not multiply the linked term by every raw TD factor. TL steps improve the base link share. Helping Hands adds independently weighted automatic tap income alongside manual taps. All coefficients and the proposed two-family first-link gate remain provisional.

Tap-level discounts reduce the price of tap levels. They do not reduce draw prices, Talent requirements, or all cash unlockables. Progress while away remains viable within the confirmed pacing targets.

## Whole-shop profit and unlockable discounts: 112 entries

Whole-shop profit gets 80 cash purchases; discounts on eligible cash-unlockable prices get 32. Their broad reach makes them potential dominant choices, so their prices, thresholds, and effect curves require comparison with targeted product purchases.

The discount affects future eligible cash quotes after purchase. It cannot refund its own purchase, lower Kaju Katli prices, or reduce real-money prices. Permanent card discounts also apply only to their declared cash category. The UI should preview the resulting effect and state the affected category.

The exact meaning of whole-shop profit, including its interaction with tapping, remains a formula decision. Avoid applying the same global bonus once through product-derived tap income and then again as a tap multiplier.

## Product links and group milestones: 48 entries

The proposed 32 directed entries connect eight product pairs, with four increasingly deep authored milestones per pair. An initial ring to compare is:

| Source product | Beneficiary |
|---|---|
| Phuljhari | Anar |
| Anar | Chakri |
| Chakri | Rockets |
| Rockets | Small crackers |
| Small crackers | Cracker strings |
| Cracker strings | Aerial shots |
| Aerial shots | Celebration boxes |
| Celebration boxes | Phuljhari |

This is a proposed dependency topology, not a claim that an eight-product ring is optimal. Each effect should derive from clear source ownership/levels or a purchased milestone. Do not recursively define product income in terms of another product's boosted income. Compare the ring with a few stronger targeted links; reject a topology that simply forces one unchanging purchase script.

The other 16 entries reward four mixed-family groupings at four milestones each. Working groups: Phuljhari/Anar/Chakri; Rockets/Aerial shots; Small crackers/Cracker strings; and Celebration boxes with a changing starter-family partner. Define each grouping's conditions and benefit explicitly when authoring individual entries. Groups may overlap, but one goal does not count twice. Avoid making every milestone require all eight products at an identical level.

These are cash-purchased unlockables whose requirements can depend on levels or ownership. If a future design makes a milestone purely automatic instead, move it out of this 1,000-purchase count and allocate a replacement entry.

## Discovery, reset, and purchase flow

The current proposal uses earning/ownership milestones for availability and cash for purchase. Calendar dates do not unlock catalog sections. Later permanent growth lets players reach new price bands across runs.

Distinguish known entries from entries owned in the current run. Cash purchases reset at prestige under the proposed reset rules; discovery records can persist. The rebuild interface should support the known portion of the catalog without presenting 1,000 simultaneous buttons. Show useful available categories, a small preview of upcoming milestones, and an accessible full catalog.

The user's latest choice makes different cash upgrades purchased at least once the lifetime component of later draw rewards. This supersedes the intervening Fame-based metric. Count each of the 1,000 catalogue entries once across all runs; rebuying after prestige does not count again. Candidate milestones at 250 / 500 / 750 / 1,000 make a bonus available on qualified runs, but these thresholds and the qualification remain unapproved. Catalogue discovery records should distinguish seen entries from purchases. The [prestige draft](Talent%20and%20Prestige%20Draft.md) keeps Fame as a separate income proposal. No additional cash purchase is created.

The confirmed controls are individual manual purchases and player-triggered Buy All, in one Upgrades screen with category and product filters. No later prestige or card enables automatic buying. The [Upgrade Browser and Goals Draft](Upgrade%20Browser%20and%20Goals%20Draft.md) now defines proposed available/upcoming/owned states, search, contribution previews, first purchases versus rebuys, and the batch review. Individual selection lets players preserve cash for a desired purchase.

The proposed batch orders eligible entries by current price, recalculating discounts and availability after each simulated purchase, and stops when its initial cash budget cannot afford another entry in scope. The review includes category/product/skill/search scope, cash cost, count, lifetime-count changes, and benefits. Confirm only the reviewed list; income earned while reviewing does not silently expand it. Material price/eligibility changes require a refreshed review. Buy All is a convenience rather than an optimal purchase strategy. It spends no Kaju Katli or draw credits and buys no product/tap levels. Detailed scope, ordering, and review behavior remain proposals.

The first run must provide regular milestones entirely through the run economy. Talent is revealed at the first prestige, so first-run balance cannot assume a paid card or an earlier free draw.

Confirmed: all eight product families are obtainable before the first prestige, with no family reserved behind a later prestige requirement. The [product progression draft](Product%20Progression%20Draft.md) sequences their reveals alongside skills, discounts, and an older-product comeback. Leave useful purchases after the final family reveal. This does not confirm an ownership gate for prestige or a specific number of first-run catalogue entries.
