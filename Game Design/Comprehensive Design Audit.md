# Rocket Raja: comprehensive game-design problem audit

**Reviewed 30 September 2026.** This is a review of the checked-in Unity implementation, authored catalog, recent 2026-09-28 desktop captures, and a freshly instrumented deterministic economy run. It extends the [economy audit](Rocket%20Raja%20Economy%20Audit.md), [Tap Tycoon comparison](Comparison%20and%20Recommendations.md), [research-paper review](Research%20Paper%20Review.md), and [incremental-game benchmarks](Incremental%20Game%20Benchmarks.md). It proposes design changes; it does not change the playable economy.

## Executive finding

Rocket Raja has a coherent, technically implemented tap → products → upgrades → skills → prestige → Legacy loop. Its main design problem is **the distribution of meaningful choices across that loop**. In the current scripted active run, all eight product families are owned at **7:31**, the last cash upgrade is purchased at **26:30**, and first prestige becomes available at **46:26**. Thus **38:55 (84%)** of the run follows the final family unlock, including **19:56** after the final purchased upgrade. Product levels still increase, but no new family or authored upgrade arrives during that last interval. This is a simulated-policy result, not an observed human session.

Three other problems compound the gap: early families lose direct contribution; tapping ceases to matter when the product economy accelerates; and the first prestige's real numerical benefit is obscured by perk order and a reset that introduces no new action. The same UI hides eligible upgrade choices behind a cheapest-ten list. Those are stronger immediate findings than a general call for more content.

### Evidence classes and severity

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Confirmed rule** | Directly read from the implemented catalog, engine, UI, or a test asserting that behavior. |
| **Measured policy** | Produced by the authored-catalog simulator under a specified automatic play policy. It is reproducible for that policy, not a player average. |
| **Observed UI** | Present in current screenshots and/or screen code. Device layout and player interpretation still need testing. |
| **Hypothesis** | Plausible effect on comprehension, enjoyment, or retention. Requires player observation or telemetry. |

**P0** means a documented acceptance target fails or the evidence base is stale. **P1** means a central choice or reward loses value. **P2** means clarity, flow, or future depth is limited. **P3** means a contingent issue to investigate before allocating production work. Severity is a design-work priority, not a claim about measured retention.

## Fresh progression trace and method

The [simulator](../Tools/EconomyLab/Program.cs#L124) advances one second at a time, taps three times per active second, calls Buy All, buys affordable product/tap levels by marginal-income payback, and activates ready skills. I copied it to a temporary diagnostic project, replaced only its two pacing-assertion throws with logging, and added read-only checkpoints. The diagnostic copy was deleted. The catalog input was the checked-in [ContentCatalog.json](../Documentation/ContentCatalog.json); the runtime's authored source is [GameContent.asset](../Assets/Resources/GameContent.asset). The unmodified `--simulate` still throws `First-run pacing target failed`.

| First active run checkpoint | Families | Purchased run upgrades / 96 | Three manual taps/s ÷ normal product income | First-family share | Last-family share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5:00 | 2 | 5 | 35.94% | 51.61% | Not owned |
| **7:31**, all families | 8 | 44 | **0.00225%** | 0.09175% | 28.23% |
| 10:00 | 8 | 79 | 0.000006324% | 0.001135% | 66.41% |
| 30:00 | 8 | 87 | 0.000002163% | 0.001364% | 82.47% |
| **46:26**, prestige | 8 | 87 | **0.000002051%** | 0.001328% | 82.76% |

These are **instantaneous potential rates**, not cash earned over an entire session. The ratio counts three manual taps each second against `NormalIncome`; the simulator's Helping Hands, Rush, and ad effects are not included in that ratio. The last purchased upgrade occurs at **26:30**. Nine expensive upgrade entries remain at prestige, so “content exhausted” here means no further *purchased* authored upgrade in the traced remainder, not all 96 owned. An exploratory rerun that stopped manual taps after all eight families were owned reached the first prestige at **46:21** versus **46:26** with continued tapping. That five-second change is specific to this automatic purchase policy; it is not a controlled estimate of tapping's value to people.

The first three current active-policy cycles are **46:26, 12:36, 9:10**, while the older checked-in [EconomySimulation.json](../Documentation/EconomySimulation.json) says **24:35, 9:18, 5:55**. No recent catalog or engine change explains the older saved result; it must not be treated as current evidence. See [Balance.md](../Documentation/Balance.md) for the original target and [Validation.md](../Documentation/Validation.md) for outstanding human tests.

## Problem register

### A. Pacing, purchases, and products

| ID / priority | Finding and evidence | Player-facing consequence to test | Design response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1 · P0** | First prestige is 46:26 against the simulator's 20–30 minute bound; second run is 12:36 against its 5–10 minute bound. **Measured policy.** [Assertion](../Tools/EconomyLab/Program.cs#L165) | The first reset may arrive after the initial novelty has worn off. | Restore a passing, versioned baseline before tuning; compare more than one purchase policy. |
| **A2 · P1** | All families open at 7:31; first reset is 38:55 later; no cash upgrade bought in the final 19:56. **Measured policy.** | A long stretch offers levels and money without a new economic decision or reveal. | Add one or two mid/late run goals or mechanics, and/or shorten this stretch. Measure time between meaningful events. |
| **A3 · P1** | Every next family's base cost and income are 8× the previous family's; milestones only boost their own family. The first family falls to 0.001328% of product income by reset in the traced run. **Confirmed rule + measured policy.** [Catalog](../Assets/Game/Core/Catalog.cs#L67), [income](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L35) | Starter products become visually and economically ignorable even though they can occasionally be efficient milestone purchases. | Test a few cross-family or portfolio milestones with explicit total-income previews. Do not require equal raw income from all families. |
| **A4 · P1** | Non-skill `requiredLevel` fields do not gate upgrades; product upgrades can be bought before their product is owned. The code test asserts this. **Confirmed rule.** [Unlock](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L133), [test](../Tools/EconomyLab/Program.cs#L65) | Named level landmarks can imply an unlock that never occurs, while purchases can have zero immediate production effect. | Decide whether these are true unlock conditions. If pre-buying remains allowed, show “applies when owned” and the current versus future effect. |
| **A5 · P1** | Player shows only the ten cheapest *remaining* upgrades, although the engine permits buying any affordable unlocked entry. Buy All buys every affordable entry cheapest first. **Confirmed UI/rule.** [Player list](../Assets/Game/Runtime/ImportedPlayerScreen.cs#L132), [Buy All](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L145) | An affordable high-value specialization can be invisible until cheaper entries are purchased; Buy All can absorb cash into unowned-family or low-impact upgrades. | Show all available upgrades with filters and contribution previews. Offer an explicit “buy affordable” action while preserving individual choice. |
| **A6 · P1** | `VisualTier` changes at 1/25/50/100, but the imported Home binds one sprite per product and one stall backdrop. Tier increases pulse the same sprite; `StallTier` has no runtime consumer. **Confirmed implementation.** [Home builder](../Assets/Game/Editor/BuildImportedHome.cs#L52), [Home refresh](../Assets/Game/Runtime/ImportedHomeScreen.cs#L195), [tier rules](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L32) | The economic milestones have feedback but do not visibly evolve the shop as prior documentation claimed. The first shelf can look the same at level 1 and 100. | Either wire actual tier/stall sprites and verify them at every milestone or remove the promise of visual tiers. Test whether the result makes progress legible. |
| **A6b · P2** | Priced profit upgrades stop at the level-250 landmark, but numeric levels continue to 100,000. Self-milestones recur every hundred. **Confirmed rule.** [Catalog](../Assets/Game/Core/Catalog.cs#L79), [milestones](../Assets/Game/Core/ProductMilestones.cs#L8) | Very high levels may yield numbers without a new authored capability or look. | Author a small number of later beats where playtests show a gap; avoid creating a long checklist of cosmetic-only milestones. |
| **A7 · P2** | The eight product rows are tall and inside a constrained scroll panel. The current desktop capture exposes roughly two rows at once; exact capacity varies by device. **Observed UI + code.** [Products builder](../Assets/Game/Editor/BuildImportedProducts.cs#L62) | Players may not notice later families or compare purchase options quickly. | Test a compact summary/list view or a pinned “next useful purchase” strip on small devices. |
| **A8 · P3** | Global Buy ×1/×10/×100/MAX mode survives an in-session prestige; fixed ×10/×100 requires the entire batch to be affordable. **Confirmed rule/UI.** [Products screen](../Assets/Game/Runtime/ImportedProductsScreen.cs#L49), [quote](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L77) | A retained ×100 mode can disable the first post-reset buy until the player notices the mode control. | Reset to ×1 at prestige or fall back to the affordable portion with explicit quantity. Observe actual confusion first. |

**Design interpretation.** [AdVenture Capitalist's documented cross-business Unlocks](https://screenzilla.helpshift.com/hc/en/5-adventure-capitalist/faq/506-unlocks-have-incorrect-descriptions/) demonstrate a clear way for an old business to have portfolio value. The [benchmarks](Incremental%20Game%20Benchmarks.md) also document Cookie Clicker's renewed uses for existing buildings. These examples justify testing a dependency, not importing their multipliers. The research review explains why new goals and visible returns plausibly matter; it does not provide a universal number of minutes between upgrades.

### B. Tapping and active skills

| ID / priority | Finding and evidence | Player-facing consequence to test | Design response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1 · P1** | Tap payout is `(1 + 1.4 × tap level) × tap upgrades × tap Legacy`. It receives none of the product milestones, eight all-product upgrades, product Legacy, or Festival Rush. The traced tap/passive rate drops from 35.94% at 5:00 to 0.00225% at 7:31. **Confirmed rule + measured policy.** [Tap formula](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L41) | The game's signature action becomes an instruction without an economic payoff. | Prototype a bounded link from normal shop production to deliberate taps, with a displayed active contribution and accessible low-tap path. |
| **B2 · P1** | Each extra tap level adds a fixed 1.4 before multipliers, while its next cost rises 17% per level. The automatic policy stops buying tap levels at 60. **Confirmed rule + policy.** [Tap cost](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L96) | Repeated tap-level buys can appear available but cease to compete with passive investments. | Show marginal ₹/tap and approximate active payback. Rework late tap progression only after observing use. |
| **B3 · P1** | Helping Hands generates tap income, so it inherits B1's late-run collapse. Festival Rush boosts products, while Bulk Order grants normal sales and does not benefit from Rush. **Confirmed rule.** [Advance](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L51), [skills](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L153) | Three skills occupy home controls yet offer little strategic interaction; a tap skill may be a weak late purchase. | After repairing tap scaling, test one legible skill combo with a hard cap and visible timing. Measure use rather than maximizing stacking by default. |
| **B4 · P2** | Home still says “Tap to earn” at high income, regardless of the current tap/passive ratio. **Confirmed UI.** [Home](../Assets/Game/Runtime/ImportedHomeScreen.cs#L227) | The prompt can overstate an ineffective action and hide a better next goal. | Make the hint contextual: show the next affordable meaningful action or an active tap burst when it has value. |
| **B5 · P3** | The scripted policy taps three times every active second for a 46-minute first run and automatically uses ready skills. **Confirmed test method.** [Simulator](../Tools/EconomyLab/Program.cs#L133) | Its active-play times can be very different from comfortable or accessible human sessions. | Add lower-tap and short-session policies; collect actual tap cadence and skill use in consented playtests. |

The [Tap Tycoon comparison](Comparison%20and%20Recommendations.md) proposes an initial linked-tap experiment with caps. [Tap Titans 2's developer design post](https://gamehive.com/blog/tt2-devlog-10-first-look-into-v20/) and [Realm Grinder's developer description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/610080/Realm_Grinder/) show games that support distinct active and passive routes. That is a design option, not evidence that every player must tap throughout a run.

### C. Prestige and permanent progression

| ID / priority | Finding and evidence | Player-facing consequence to test | Design response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 · P1** | At the first reset, the player normally receives five reputation. Legacy lists eight product-specific perks before global product and tap perks, with no predicted income gain. **Confirmed UI/rule.** [Catalog](../Assets/Game/Core/Catalog.cs#L73), [Legacy screen](../Assets/Game/Runtime/ImportedLegacyScreen.cs#L65) | A novice can buy what appears first rather than what accelerates their next run. At equal level-one products and equal multipliers, spending one point on each of the first five **listed** family perks adds only **0.1953%** total normal income; spending three on global product expertise doubles it. This is a state example, not a full-run value comparison; early-family ranks can still help the early rebuild. | Reorder/group by effect, show the projected gain at matched early and late checkpoints, and recommend an affordable first purchase without forcing it. |
| **C2 · P1** | Prestige resets the same product and upgrade sequence; it unlocks no new action, family, or permanent appearance. The first Legacy purchase can make a faster second run, but only changes numbers. **Confirmed rule + measured policy.** [Prestige](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L169), [catalog](../Assets/Game/Core/Catalog.cs#L73) | “Do it again faster” may not be enough to make the reset feel like an achievement. | Add one persistent, comprehensible choice or ability after a proven first-reset preview. Then test whether the second run actually feels different. |
| **C3 · P1** | Reward is `floor(5 × sqrt(eligible earnings / threshold))`, with a hard five-point jump at eligibility. The sixth point requires **1.44×** the threshold; ten points require **4×**. The panel shows the present reward and gate but not the next-point requirement or estimated wait. **Confirmed rule/UI.** [Preview](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L163), [panel](../Assets/Game/Runtime/ImportedPrestigeScreen.cs#L68) | Players cannot judge whether to reset now or push further, especially across long gaps between integer rewards. | Show “next point at X eligible earnings” and compare a reset-now versus push estimate under a named policy; do not present the estimate as a promise. |
| **C4 · P2** | The fixed 60 Legacy ranks cost **434** reputation total; after they are all bought, reputation has no other implemented use, while prestige can keep awarding it. **Confirmed rule.** [Catalog](../Assets/Game/Core/Catalog.cs#L73), [Legacy purchase](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L183) | The long-term reward loses utility if players reach the cap. Its actual reachability is unmeasured. | Instrument cap approach before authoring a diminishing late sink or new persistent goal. Do not add infinite flat multiplication. |
| **C5 · P2** | The prestige panel uses **₹170Q** for the gate while eligible earnings use the `Amount` suffix **170aa** for the same magnitude. **Confirmed UI.** [Panel](../Assets/Game/Runtime/ImportedPrestigeScreen.cs#L76), [formatter](../Assets/Game/Core/Amount.cs#L63) | The player may interpret the requirement and progress as different units. | Use one amount formatter and a progress bar toward the current/next reward. |
| **C6 · P2** | Ready prestige gets a brief home notice; the persistent entry point is on Player. **Confirmed UI.** [Home notice](../Assets/Game/Runtime/ImportedHomeScreen.cs#L236), [Player button](../Assets/Game/Runtime/ImportedPlayerScreen.cs#L130) | The central reset may be easy to overlook during normal home/product play. | Keep a persistent ready indicator until the prestige screen is opened; test notice comprehension. |
| **C7 · P3** | Offline-cap and cooldown Legacy ranks cost the same first point as ×2 product/tap specializations, but offline-cap does nothing for absences ≤8 hours and cooldown only helps repeated skill activation. **Confirmed rule; value depends on behavior.** [Engine](../Assets/Game/Core/GameEngine.cs#L44) | Contextual perks can look equal in price but vary widely in usefulness. | Describe their expected use case and show an estimated benefit for that player's pattern; tune prices only with use data. |

Prestige is already mechanically useful: the traced second active run takes 12:36 rather than 46:26 after the first Legacy spending policy. The concern is **visibility, choice, and replay variety**, not absence of a multiplier. [Tap Titans 2's official prestige FAQ](https://gamehive.helpshift.com/hc/en/3-tap-titans-2/faq/75-should-i-prestige-when/) shows a reset that discloses what is kept, a higher starting state, and the role of persistent Artifacts. [Idle Miner Tycoon's official prestige help](https://kolibri-games.helpshift.com/hc/en/3-idle-miner-tycoon/faq/48-how-does-the-prestige-system-work/) similarly explains its retained resources and rebuild cost. Rocket Raja can apply the disclosure principle without copying those economies.

The 50% offline rate and eight-hour base cap are clear rules, not inherently bad balance. After a 16-hour absence, the base payout equals four hours of current normal production; the After-hours perk can raise the cap. Whether that return feels generous depends on the target play pattern and the surrounding goals. The [paper review](Research%20Paper%20Review.md) distinguishes evidence that players value background progress from any unsupported claim that a particular percentage is ideal.

## Causal chains worth testing

1. **Newest family dominates → old family feels pointless.** The 8× ladder makes direct shares collapse. Self-only milestones do not change portfolio opportunity cost. A cross-family bonus could make an old-family purchase valuable even when its own row remains small. Test *total* marginal portfolio gain, not only the old-family's direct share.
2. **Automatic income compounds → tapping loses agency.** Products receive more multiplicative layers than taps. Helping Hands inherits that weak tap value. A bounded link to normal production would preserve tap relevance without requiring constant high-speed tapping. Test active contribution and accessibility together.
3. **Long first run → prestige arrives after content slows.** All families appear early, upgrades stop arriving by 26:30 in the scripted run, and the gate arrives at 46:26. A lower gate alone may help time-to-reset but could leave the weak tap and starter-family problems untouched. Test cadence and reward comprehension separately.
4. **Prestige reward exists → player may still miss its best use.** Five points can buy the three-point global ×2 perk, but that row follows eight specific perks. A forecast and category grouping can expose a stronger choice without changing any reward number.
5. **More systems → more hidden choices if navigation stays fixed.** The cheapest-ten Player pool already masks the 96-entry catalog. New upgrades, goals, or specializations need discoverable filters and clear previews before they are added.

## What this audit cannot establish

- No repository evidence measures real retention, churn, payer behavior, average tap cadence, first-session comprehension, or whether players prefer a 20–30 minute first prestige. The existing targets are design intentions.
- Desktop screenshots do not prove layout on physical Android screens, cutouts, or all font scales. The current UI findings are code-backed where possible.
- The simulator's automatic Buy All and best-payback purchasing may be faster, slower, or simply different from humans. It cannot be treated as a representative player sample.
- Academic papers and competitor mechanics can suggest *hypotheses and design structures*. None establishes that importing a particular multiplier or feature will improve Rocket Raja retention.

The [Design Recovery Roadmap](Design%20Recovery%20Roadmap.md) turns this register into a measurement and experiment sequence. Each proposed economy change should keep the authored asset, export, simulation, UI copy, and save migration in sync.
